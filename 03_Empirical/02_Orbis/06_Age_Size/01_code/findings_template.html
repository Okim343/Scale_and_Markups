Orbis Spain · firm-level evidence · 2015–2023

Firm age, firm size, capital intensity and outsourcing

The hypothesis under test: young firms hold more capital and produce more in-house, while larger and older firms outsource more and hold less capital. Firm age is measured from incorporation dates. All comparisons are within 4-digit NAICS industry and year. The size half of the hypothesis is supported; the age half is not, once firms of the same size are compared.

At a given size, newly incorporated firms hold markedly less capital, per euro of sales and per worker, and the gap closes gradually over the first two decades. They also buy in a slightly larger share of their inputs. Without the size control the materials-share gap changes sign: young firms appear more vertically integrated only because they are small.

The materials share rises monotonically with size, and capital per euro of sales falls. Per worker, larger firms hold more capital, so the size result concerns capital relative to output rather than capital deepening. Holding age fixed leaves the profiles almost unchanged.

The materials share varies almost entirely across columns, that is, with size. Capital per euro of sales varies along both dimensions: it falls with size and rises with age, so young, large firms are the least capital-intensive group.

The capital result holds in every sector: young firms and large firms both hold less capital per euro of sales. The age gap in the materials share is positive in 13 of 14 sectors (agriculture is the exception) and small next to the size gap, which is positive everywhere except accommodation and food.

Note:

Measures

Materials share: materials and purchased inputs over sales. In Spanish accounts this line (aprovisionamientos) includes subcontracted work, so a higher share means more of the product is bought rather than made. Capital: book tangible fixed assets. Age: closing year minus year of incorporation.

Sample and estimation

Unconsolidated accounts, 2015–2023, the 14 NAICS sectors retained for production-function estimation, with positive sales, materials, wage bill and capital and a materials share in [0, 1]. A regression of each outcome on age groups and revenue deciles with industry × year fixed effects and firm-clustered errors gives the same signs; all age coefficients are many standard errors from zero. Within firms born since 2010, doubling sales raises the materials share by about 1.4pp and lowers capital per euro of sales by about 33%.

Caveats

Book values understate old firms' capital (historical cost, net of depreciation), which biases the age gap towards zero. Leasing: under Spanish GAAP operating leases are off balance sheet, so young firms that rent premises and equipment show low measured capital. Selection: old firms are the survivors of their cohorts, so the age profile mixes ageing with exit.
