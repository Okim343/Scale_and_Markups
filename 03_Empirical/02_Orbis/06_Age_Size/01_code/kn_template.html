The corporate labour share in Spanish firm data, read against Karabarbounis and Neiman (2014)

Karabarbounis and Neiman (KN) document a global decline in the corporate labour share and attribute about half of it to cheaper investment goods, with an elasticity of substitution between capital and labour of about 1.25. This note puts our firm-level data on KN's footing: the same labour-share concept, the same income accounting, and the same estimating equation. It asks what the data can add to their argument and where they cannot be used to test it.

The average firm pays out about of its value added in wages, but the aggregate share is about : the covariance term is about points in every year. The aggregate KN study is therefore set by the large firms. A decline in it can come from falling labour shares within firms or from activity moving towards large, low-share firms. KN's within-industry finding does not separate the two, because reallocation across firms within an industry is still within the industry.

Table 2

What these data can and cannot say about KN

Can say

Level and short-run movements. Orbis comes within 2–5 points of the national-accounts corporate labour share from 2020, and the balanced panel reproduces its 2020 jump. Orbis covers 53–60% of corporate value added from 2016 (43% in 2015).
Aggregation. The aggregate is roughly 15 points below the typical firm's labour share; large firms set it.
Accounting by size. Under KN's own identity, the lower labour share of large firms is almost entirely the markup term, for either rental rate.
KN's channel in this window. Spanish investment prices rose relative to consumption, so the capital-price channel cannot have lowered the labour share after 2015.

Cannot say

The elasticity σ. Nine years and 16 sections give an interval far too wide; KN use 15–37-year trends across up to 129 country-industry pairs.
The trend in the labour share. Changing Orbis coverage moves the full-sample trend; the balanced panel is not representative of entry.
Markup levels. They depend on R, on book capital (historical cost, land) and on leased capital being off the balance sheet.
Technology versus market power. A cross-section cannot separate capital-augmenting technology or σ from markups; the size result is an accounting statement, not a causal one.

Note:

Sample

Spanish unconsolidated accounts 2015–2023, all non-financial industries (finance and public administration excluded), nature-of-expense accounts with reported depreciation: firm-years, firms. Firm-level shares (Figure 2a) use firms with positive value added and trim the 1% tails of the labour share.

Value added

Gross operating value added = operating revenue − materials − other external costs (Orbis other operating expenses net of staff costs and depreciation). It excludes other taxes on production, which sit in other external costs; in the national accounts, moving to factor cost changes the labour share by less than 0.5 points.

Estimation

Trends are OLS slopes on year, per decade. Figure 4 reports value-added-weighted and unweighted least squares with heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors; KN use robust regression across countries. KN's predicted change is (1 − sL)(σ − 1) times the investment-price trend.
